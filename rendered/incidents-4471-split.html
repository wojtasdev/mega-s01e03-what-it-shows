The table’s own footnote already says it: 5 of the reports filed under parser were read by the desk as a one-day move, not a month/day swap — see desk-notes.json. Read their bodies and it holds: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting. We held it twice.” is a timezone/day-boundary bug, not a transposed date. It has never had a ticket of its own.

Three problems, not one

  1. parser — the shared parser reads every date month-first; any day-first tenant gets it flipped, on any channel, unless the hint bug below already explains the report. 374 reports, 11 tenants. → issue
  2. hint — booking-v3’s on-screen date hint says MM/DD/YYYY no matter the tenant’s own locale; day-first-locale people read the hint and type the date month-first by hand, and the form stores exactly what they typed. A UI/copy bug, not a parsing bug — needs a different fix. 33 reports, 3 tenants (all three on booking-v3). → issue
  3. one-day move — not a date-order bug at all: a one-day shift, timezone-shaped. Filed under parser by the mechanical tenant+channel classifier because nothing else was looking for it. 5 reports. → issue
374 + 33 + 5 = 412 of #4471's 412. One ticket, one fix, does not close all three.

Lessons

  • A ticket’s own footnote can hold the next ticket. Read it before you close the big one.
  • “Known” described the loudest bug, not the only one.
  • The automatic classifier (tenant + channel) is a good first cut, not a verdict — it has no way to see a report that is about something else entirely.
